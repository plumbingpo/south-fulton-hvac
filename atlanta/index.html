When Your System Fails in Atlanta

If your heat or air quits, how fast help arrives matters more than anything else. For that reason, we keep our schedule flexible across the area and answer every call with a live person. Urgent calls usually get a technician within a day, and we treat a failed furnace in a cold snap or a dead AC in the heat ahead of routine work.

The urgent calls we answer tend to follow a pattern. On the cooling side, it's warm air from the vents, an outdoor unit that hums without starting, or an evaporator coil frozen solid. On the heating side, it's a furnace that lights and then shuts down. Year-round, it's breakers that trip every cycle, plugged condensate drains overflowing into the furnace cabinet, and thermostats that stop responding. Because our trucks are stocked with capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and fittings, the majority of these calls get fixed on the first trip, at a price you approve before we start.

A few minutes of caution while you wait for us can head off a much larger repair. A frozen air conditioner should be shut off at the thermostat with the fan set to "on", which lets the coil thaw faster. Running it frozen can flood the drain pan and damage the compressor. A short-cycling furnace, or any burning smell, means it's time to kill power at the furnace switch and call us immediately.

Keeping Your Atlanta HVAC System Running Longer

Most mid-season breakdowns start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Swap your filter every one to three months, keep plants and shrubs at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and hose off the condenser coil once a year in spring. Then book a professional tune-up each spring and fall, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular service, central air typically lasts 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rising energy bills, rooms that won't reach the setpoint, or repeat service calls, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and is far less stressful than an emergency swap during the worst heat wave of the year.

If comfort problems do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A weak capacitor is a quick fix. The same capacitor left alone for a season can take the compressor with it and turn a minor repair into a full system replacement. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and sleepless nights.
